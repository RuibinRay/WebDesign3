Ripeness is the recipe.

Racimo is a plantain chip brand from Talamanca, Costa Rica. This page explains every decision in the draft: the idea, the name, the range, the colours, the type, what moves and why, and what is still to be generated.

01The idea

A plantain is four different foods depending on the day you cut it. Racimo sells that, and nothing else.

Every plantain chip brand says the same things: crunchy, natural, tropical. None of them is built on the one fact every Costa Rican cook already knows, which is that a green plantain and a black one behave like different vegetables. Green is starch: it fries pale and snaps. Black is sugar: it caramelises and chews. The range, the page, the pack and the pictures all come from that single scale of twelve days.

So the site ripens. The hero bunch goes from green to black as you scroll. The page colour follows the peel. Each bag is the colour of its day and carries a day stamp. The words on the buttons are the only place the brand raises its voice.

02The name

Racimo

A racimo is a bunch of plantains, the whole stalk as it is cut from the plant. It is short, it sounds like what it is in Spanish, an English speaker can say it (rah-SEE-moh), and it holds the idea: everything in the range comes off one bunch.

A web search on 2026-11-20 found no plantain chip brand with this name. That is a quick check, not a trademark clearance.

The four products are named with the words already used for the fruit at each stage: Verde (green), Pintón (turning, literally “painted”), Maduro (ripe) and Miel (honey, for the black stage when the fruit is almost syrup).

Each day has its own cut for a reason. Green fruit is hard enough to slice into long thin ribbons. Ripe fruit is too soft for that, so it is cut thicker and on the bias, and the black stage is cut into thick rounds and dried before it is fried.

04The colours

The palette is the peel. Four colours are the four days and are used as full flat backgrounds, never as tints. Leaf green is the plant before day 1 and opens and closes the page. Paper and ink carry the reading. One red, taken from the painted oxcarts of Sarchí, is kept for the day stamp, the small dot before each section label, and hover.

The four peel colours are taken from the photographs. Once the pictures existed, each backdrop was sampled and the site’s green, yellow-green, gold and black-brown were set to those values. A photo, the drawn bag and the section behind them are now one colour, so nothing sits in a visible box.

How the colour is finished. Between two days the page colour is mixed in a perceptual colour space, so green turns to yellow through a bright yellow-green and not through olive. Every colour world has a soft pool of light behind its subject. A fine print grain lies over the whole page, photographs and films included, so flat colour reads as ink on paper. Where two colours meet, the edge is cut in small teeth like the crimped end of a chip bag. Amber, the colour of the frying oil, takes the section that follows the kettle.

Bricolage Grotesque, set narrow and heavy, for names and headlines: it has the weight of a hand-painted pulpería sign without imitating one. The same family at its regular width is the reading face, so the page uses one voice at two volumes.

DM Mono · días · labels · numbers · 01 05 09 12

DM Mono is for anything that reads like a stamp or a ledger: the day counter, labels, data. Spanish is used where the Spanish word is the real name of the thing (racimo, pintón, paila, con qué) and is always explained once.

06Drawn in code

Everything below is drawn by js/art.js from js/data.js. Because of that the site is complete before any photograph exists: where a photo is missing, the right drawing stands in on the right colour.

The round chip and the painted wheel are one drawing. A slice of plantain shows a three-part core; an oxcart wheel from Sarchí is a ring of points around a flower. Put together they make the roundel on every bag.

Every bag is real-time 3D. js/bag3d.js builds a pillow bag in code (two shells, sealed flat at the top and bottom, fuller at the bottom where chips settle) and wraps it in the same drawing, front and back. It gets an ink outline and a flat shadow so it sits in the drawn world. One WebGL renderer serves all the bags on a page and only draws the ones on screen. The drawn bag shows first and stays wherever WebGL is missing.

The pack is never invented by the image model. Each bag front is saved from refs.html as a PNG and passed to Higgsfield as the reference image for every shot that shows a bag, so the label in the photographs is this design, with its real lettering.

10What is invented

Racimo does not exist. These are placeholders written for the design and must be replaced with real ones before anything is published: the day numbers (1, 5, 9, 12) and the waiting days; prices and pack weights; cut thicknesses and frying notes; all nutrition figures; the use of coconut oil; cacao on Miel; everything said about growers, kettles and the box subscription.

What is true and was kept deliberately general: Talamanca is in Limón province on the Caribbean side by the Panama border; the Sixaola river runs along that border; plantain grows year-round; a plantain’s starch turns to sugar as it ripens; in Costa Rica “chimichurri” means chopped tomato, onion and cilantro. The map is a sketch from rough coordinates and is not for navigation.
